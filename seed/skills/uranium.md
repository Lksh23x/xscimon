Score HIGH (0.7–1.0) for posts reporting or credibly discussing:

    A new, material event or confirmed development involving uranium supply, demand, pricing, policy, or the nuclear fuel cycle.
    Mine supply changes: new discoveries or resource estimates; production starts, expansions, cuts, suspensions, or shutdowns; operational problems; labor disputes; cost or recovery changes.
    Supply disruptions or additions: export restrictions, sanctions, trade restrictions, transport issues, new supply agreements, or the return of idled production.
    Uranium-market transactions: spot or term purchases, sales, tenders, contract awards, long-term supply agreements, or disclosed contracting volumes and prices.
    Price and market data: meaningful uranium spot or term-price moves, market liquidity, inventories, contracting activity, or other specific, sourced figures.
    Demand changes: utility procurement or reactor plans that materially affect uranium requirements; reactor construction, restarts, lifetime extensions, cancellations, or closures.
    Fuel-cycle developments: changes affecting uranium conversion, enrichment, fuel fabrication, or availability of nuclear fuel that could materially affect uranium demand or supply.
    Government or regulatory actions with a clear uranium-market impact, such as nuclear policy, mine approvals or denials, export controls, strategic stockpiling, or sanctions.
    Company results or guidance with uranium-specific production, costs, resources, contracts, financing, or project milestones.
    A credible report of an accident, security issue, or geopolitical event affecting uranium production, transport, nuclear-fuel supply, or reactor demand.
    A specific, substantive analysis that connects evidence to a plausible bullish or bearish uranium-market implication.
    A primary document or credible source containing material information: company filings, regulator or government statements, utility tenders, contract announcements, or clearly sourced market data.

Score MEDIUM (0.3–0.6) for:

    Informed commentary, analysis, or synthesis of uranium-market developments without a new material fact.
    Market forecasts, price targets, or supply-demand scenarios that state their assumptions and evidence.
    A meaningful project, permitting, financing, or construction update that is preliminary or has uncertain impact.
    Conference, investor-call, or policy-event posts with substantive uranium-market content.
    Expert discussion or questions that show domain knowledge but do not report a concrete development.
    Broader nuclear-energy news with a plausible but indirect connection to uranium demand.

Score LOW (0.0–0.2) for:

    Congratulatory or social replies (“great work,” “congrats,” “love this”).
    General nuclear-energy enthusiasm or opposition with no specific uranium-market connection.
    Vague bullish or bearish claims (“uranium is going to the moon,” “the sector is dead”) without evidence or new information.
    Price screenshots or predictions with no source, context, or substantive explanation.
    Promotional posts that repeat company claims without new details or verifiable milestones.
    Retweets, quotes, or headlines that add no original information or useful context.
    Recycled news, old announcements presented as new, or unsourced rumors.
    Personal, lifestyle, political, or unrelated financial-market content.

Direction and materiality

For each relevant post, identify the likely implication as bullish, bearish, mixed/unclear, or not market-directional. Don’t infer direction from tone alone. For example, a new mine may add supply, while a mine delay may tighten it; a reactor announcement may support demand, while a cancellation may reduce it. State the reason briefly.
Breaking-news handling

    Give extra priority to new, time-sensitive, consequential developments, but don’t score a post high merely because it says “breaking.”
    Prefer primary sources and posts that link to documents, named sources, or verifiable data.
    Treat unconfirmed claims as unverified; score based on the potential significance and the reliability of the evidence.
    Distinguish the date of the event from the date the post was published. Flag old news reposted as new.
    When relevant, note the affected company, country, part of the fuel cycle, and whether the impact is confirmed or only possible.

Output: relevance score (0.0–1.0), direction, key claim, and a one-sentence reason for the score.
